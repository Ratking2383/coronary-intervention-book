# Vascular Access

## Purpose

Vascular access establishes a controlled route from the skin to the arterial system for diagnostic and interventional equipment.

## Main access routes

### Radial access

Radial access is widely used because it generally reduces access-site bleeding and permits earlier mobilization. Relevant considerations include vessel size, spasm, tortuosity, prior radial procedures, and hand circulation.

### Femoral access

Femoral access remains important when larger devices, mechanical circulatory support, or additional catheter support is required. Potential complications include hematoma, retroperitoneal bleeding, pseudoaneurysm, arteriovenous fistula, and limb ischemia.

## Materials

- Access needle or micropuncture needle
- Access guidewire
- Introducer sheath and dilator
- Hemostatic valve and side arm
- Pressure tubing and transducer
- Radial compression or femoral closure device

## Safety principles

Use ultrasound and fluoroscopic guidance when appropriate, minimize traumatic attempts, select the smallest suitable sheath, and monitor the access site and distal perfusion after the procedure.
